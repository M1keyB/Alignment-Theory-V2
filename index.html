Alignment Theory

Alignment Theory studies how adaptive systems preserve coherent agency under constraint.

Healthy systems preserve the capacities required for meaningful participation, judgment, adaptation, refusal, responsibility, and action. Support can strengthen those capacities or substitute for them. Constraints can preserve usable agency or capture it. Authority determines who may legitimately bind action, and continuity determines whether meaning and authority survive handoffs over time.

Support versus substitution remains a central mechanism within this broader theory. The design problem is how to prevent capability, speed, and scale from outrunning the coherence required to govern them.

Working conceptual and design framework by Michael Nathan Bower. Canonical synthesis updated September 14, 2026; not peer reviewed.
