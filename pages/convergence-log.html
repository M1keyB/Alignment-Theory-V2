How to read this log

Independent work can converge on related questions without proving Alignment Theory or endorsing HAPI or AGS. The mappings below are this project's interpretations. They identify comparisons to test, not priority, ownership, or technical validation.

Entries distinguish conceptual overlap, source evidence, chronology, and influence. Publication dates alone cannot establish independent development. Source content was checked on September 14, 2026; empirical claims within these essays have not been independently validated here.

2026-08-16 · Boundaries and usable freedom

Source: Beth Hawks, “Don't Fear Classroom Rules - Boundaries Enable Freedom,” On the Rabbit Trail, August archive. Domain: education and practical philosophy.

Overlap and mapping: Hawks argues that classroom rules and bounded choices can make purposeful activity possible. This invites comparison with agency-preserving constraints and usable agency.

Limits and evidence strength: A conceptual comparison grounded in practitioner commentary, not empirical validation or an authority model for AGS. Hawks explicitly says the playground study story she recounts could be apocryphal; it is not treated here as research evidence.

Chronology and influence: Published after the May framework center and before the September synthesis. Whether it predates the project's first formulation of this particular overlap is unresolved. No evidence of direct influence was identified in the material reviewed; independence is not established.

2026-07-26 · Choice and novice capacity

Source: Beth Hawks, “Give them ONE way to do it,” On the Rabbit Trail. Domain: teaching and learning.

Overlap and mapping: Hawks argues that multiple methods can overwhelm novice learners. The comparison concerns the distinction between nominal options and usable agency, and scaffolding suited to developing capacity.

Limits and evidence strength: Practitioner reasoning and illustration. This does not establish that limiting choice always helps, validate the theory across domains, or justify arbitrary authority.

Chronology and influence: After the May center and before the September synthesis. Priority for this particular overlap is unresolved. No direct influence was identified in the reviewed material; the comparison does not establish genealogy.

2026-05-31 · Babel and technological ambition

Source: Beth Hawks, “It's Not Going Anywhere - The Technology Tower of Babel,” On the Rabbit Trail; content checked in the author's AI archive. Domain: theological and ethical interpretation.

Overlap and mapping: Hawks uses Babel to question technological ambition and the assumption that adoption is inevitable. The parallel concerns capability, limits, and judgment.

Limits and evidence strength: Interpretive analogy only. Her decision to abstain from generative AI differs from this project's governed-delegation approach. This is neither an endorsement of AGS nor scientific proof. Outside incident claims in the essay are not adopted here as evidence.

Chronology and influence: After the May center and before the September synthesis; the chronology of the project's Babel formulation is not established by this comparison. No direct influence was identified in the reviewed material.

2025-12-14 · Professional judgment

Source: Beth Hawks, “Professional Judgment - Don't Trade It In,” in the author's AI archive. Domain: professional education practice.

Overlap and mapping: The essay questions pressure to surrender experienced professional judgment, including to AI. The comparison concerns participation in judgment and substitution risk.

Limits and evidence strength: Practitioner commentary, not a measured test of capacity loss or runtime governance.

Chronology and influence: Predates the May 6 center and September synthesis. Whether it predates the project's earliest relevant publication is unresolved. No direct influence was identified in the material reviewed. Attribution of the overlap does not transfer ownership of either author's concepts.
