Definitions

Current Alignment Theory vocabulary for coherent agency under constraint.

Michael Nathan Bower · Updated September 14, 2026 · Working conceptual and design framework; not peer reviewed.

September 2026 definitions

Adaptive system
A system that changes behavior or state in response to environment, feedback, internal condition, external support, or accumulated history.
Agency
The usable capacity to understand, judge, refuse, revise, choose, direct action, learn from consequence, and remain responsible within a system that affects real outcomes.
Alignment
The preservation of coherent agency under constraint.
Authority
The legitimate scope to bind action, impose constraints, delegate responsibility, approve risk, or create consequence.
Capacity-forming function
A function in which continued meaningful participation helps maintain the ability to perform, judge, regulate, or adapt within that function.
Coherence
The degree to which meaning, intent, authority, action, and consequence remain mutually consistent rather than fragmenting under pressure.
Constraint
A boundary on possible action created by reality, capacity, rules, authority, environment, timing, permissions, or consequence.
Delegated agency
Operational action performed through a tool, agent, subordinate system, or workflow under borrowed human or organizational authority.
Dependency capture
A condition in which reliance on a support system becomes strong enough that refusal, exit, correction, or independent action becomes impractical.
False gate
A review or control point that blocks or permits without rightful authority, meaningful power to change the outcome, or reality-grounded conditions.
Governance Memory
A controlled history-analysis layer that identifies recurring governance patterns and recommends improvements for human review without silently rewriting authority or policy.
Handoff Integrity
The requirement that source lineage, validated context, authority scope, transformation history, expiration conditions, and proof survive transfers between agents or system stages.
Human agency infrastructure
Systems designed to preserve and amplify human capacity, judgment, authority, refusal, responsibility, and accountability while using powerful support or automation.
Internalization
The maturation process by which initially external constraints become increasingly carried through integrated understanding, skill, judgment, and governed habit.
Live human authority
Human authority that remains practically capable of changing, refusing, revising, halting, or escalating an action before consequence becomes binding.
Participatory capacity
The retained ability to remain meaningfully involved in the functions that shape judgment, responsibility, adaptation, learning, and action.
Runtime continuity
The preservation of authorization from permit to actual execution so the executed action remains the authorized action.
Semantic continuity
The preservation of meaning, source lineage, context, uncertainty, and transformation history across handoffs.
Substitution
A support relation that carries a capacity-forming function in the participant's place in a way that can preserve short-term output while weakening long-term participation or capacity.
Support
A relation that helps a person or system carry more reality with usable capacity while preserving or deepening participation in capacity-forming functions.
True constraint
A reality-grounded, purpose-congruent, authority-valid boundary that helps preserve meaningful agency. This is a framework category for analysis, not a claim that legitimacy can always be determined automatically.
True gate
A consequential decision boundary at which rightful authority can govern passage by halting, revising, refusing, approving, or escalating before consequence.

Earlier definitions: May 6, 2026

The earlier vocabulary remains below for provenance. Where a term appears in both sections, the September definition above is current. Internal and external alignment remain useful developmental concepts within the broader framework.

Read the preserved May definitions
Alignment
The state in which cognition, agency, behavior, and constraint remain coherent under pressure.
Internal Alignment
A condition where behavior and cognition are regulated by integrated understanding, conscience, coherence, and agency rather than primarily by external pressure.
External Alignment
A condition where behavior is regulated primarily by pressure, fear, reward, surveillance, institutional demand, social approval, or forced compliance.
Coherence
The preservation of meaningful structure across thought, behavior, identity, and action.
Fragmentation
The breakdown of coherence into disconnected, reactive, contradictory, or externally controlled parts.
Overload
A condition where demand exceeds integration capacity for long enough that the system begins to compensate.
Integration Capacity
The amount of complexity, uncertainty, emotional load, contradiction, or pressure a system can metabolize without losing coherence.
Signal Override
The repeated dismissal, suppression, or replacement of internal regulatory signals such as fatigue, discomfort, conscience, doubt, confusion, pain, or moral tension.
Signal Authority Loss
The process by which internal signals lose influence after being repeatedly overridden.
Identity Hardening
A compensatory response to overload in which identity becomes more rigid in order to reduce uncertainty, complexity, and regulatory demand.
External Control
The use of force, pressure, surveillance, reward, punishment, status, or authority to produce behavior without requiring internal integration.
Internal Regulation
The capacity of a system to guide behavior through integrated understanding, values, conscience, reflection, and adaptive self-correction.
Recovery
The restoration of regulatory capacity, signal sensitivity, coherence, and integration after load.
Slack
Unused regulatory capacity that allows a system to absorb stress, integrate complexity, and correct course without collapse.
Constraint
A recurring structural limit that determines what a system can sustain without losing coherence.
Constraint Fidelity
The degree to which a system preserves its governing constraints under pressure, optimization, or changing conditions.
Meaning Formation
The emergence of lived meaning through voluntary, coherent integration rather than forced compliance.
